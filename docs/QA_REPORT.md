v1.1.0 through v1.3.0 change only the app (no question or card content). v1.2.1 fixes the empty exam-date field overflowing the screen on an iPhone (found on a real device, in the Home Screen app; Linux WebKit doesn't show it). v1.3.0 makes studying more rewarding: celebrations, a study-days streak, a daily-goal card, and a review that is easy to start, resume and finish. ## Are the keys right? (blind check)

No human expert has reviewed this bank. Every check below was done by AI models, some from the same family that wrote the items, so shared mistakes can survive.

| Run | Reader | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Round 1 and round 2 drafts | Opus, told the main ethics rules, real item ids | 361/361 and 362/362. Superseded: those items were rewritten |
| **Final bank (364), anonymous ids, no hints** | **Opus** (same family as the writers) | **364/364** |
| **Same** | **Sonnet** (different model) | **348/364 = 95.6%** (±2.1) |

The brief's bar is 95%. Sonnet's number is the more independent one and clears it narrowly.

After that run, 22 items were edited for disagreement, low confidence, or factual/safety notes, and two that drew doubt twice (H-III-004, H-III-008) were replaced. Rechecks: Opus 32/32 and 4/4; Sonnet 30/32 and 3/4. Asking the same model again is weak evidence, so it doesn't change the headline. The last miss (D-II-001) came from an ambiguous quote, since reworded and not re-run. Eight items Sonnet missed were left unedited because the stem decides them: G-II-001, I-I-005, C-I-001, L-I-019, L-I-017, I-IV-032, I-II-003, G-III-015. E-VI-012 was answered correctly but with low confidence. **Treat these, and L-I-014, as the first ones to question.**

Reviewer transcripts were searched for access to keys, the repository, or other packets. None was found (four matches were false positives). Reviewers were kept away by instruction, not by a sandbox.

By knowledge group, Haiku ranged from 8% (J) to 42% (M, 14 items); B (38%) and M have the most remaining cueing.

## Flash drill (v1.4.0)

Why: learners were surprised how scenario-heavy the app is. The flash drill adds short rounds of multiple-choice and true/false recall facts, kept apart from every exam-question record (see DECISIONS).

**Plan review.** Claude Opus 5.5, GPT-6.2 Sol and Gemini 4 Neon reviewed the plan (`docs/FLASH_PLAN.md`) read-only, against the code, at high effort.

| Round | GPT-6.2 Sol | Claude Opus 5.5 | Gemini 4 Neon |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 6 | 7 | 7 |
| 2 | 7 | 8 | 8 |

Round 2's required items (a replace marker for stale rounds, deep validation of stored and restored flash data, practice-ahead never raising a level, the card opening in a dialog, a `correction` field, a flash-aware Progress, targeted comparisons in the tests) were folded into the plan's revision 3 and implemented.

**Content.** 222 facts (155 multiple choice, 67 true/false: 34 true, 33 false), 9–44 per knowledge group by blueprint weight, at most 3 per concept card, each grounded in one card and citing that card's refs. Written by six Claude Opus writers to `docs/FLASH_WRITING_GUIDE.md`; checked by `validate-bank.mjs` (schema, card/KG match, cueing, balance, near-duplicates).

| Check | Pass 1 (226 facts) | Pass 2 (102 revised facts) |
|---|---|---|
| Blind answer, Gemini 4 Neon (key agreement) | 226/226 (100%) | 102/102 (100%) |
| Options only, Gemini 4 Neon (key picked without the prompt) | 129/167 (77%) | 72/81 (89%) |
| Source support vs the cards, GPT-6.2 Sol | 1 wrong, 12 ambiguous, 15 unsupported, 27 minor | 0 wrong, 4 ambiguous, 15 unsupported, 6 minor |

After pass 1 the writers fixed every flagged fact and rewrote 71 facts whose options gave the key away (one sound option among three poor ones) so all options come from the same family; one fact was dropped. After pass 2 the three facts still ambiguous were dropped and the rest corrected as the reviewer suggested. The options-only rate stays high: for recall facts an expert can often recognize the textbook principle without the question, so it is reported, not chased.

**Data safety proof.**
- Unit: a v1.3.0-shaped device (IndexedDB-like and localStorage fallback) loads every part unchanged with nothing written; a grade writes only the flash key; malformed stored or restored flash data is ignored without blocking the rest; v1.3.0 backups restore as before; reset and restore stop an open round.
- Browser (WebKit, 4 sizes): after real study, a full flash round (with a miss, its retry, a guess, the card dialog and keyboard answers) leaves every other IndexedDB key byte-identical, and the Today goal line and the whole Progress page (minus the flash tile) unchanged.
- Upgrade: the committed v1.3.0 build is served and updated to v1.4.0 through the real service worker with a timed block mid-answer and a second tab still on v1.3.0: the update is not offered while the block runs; after it, every key is byte-identical and the block's result reads the same; a flash round then changes only the flash key; the old tab's writes leave flash intact; the drill works offline.
- The compiled exam items, cards, blueprint and refs are identical to v1.3.0's.

**Accessibility.** The flash setup, question, verdict, card dialog and summary pass axe (WCAG 2.1 A/AA, serious and critical) in Chromium, light and dark; WebKit can't run axe (see the a11y spec).

## Known limitations

- **No human expert review**, and all blind-check figures are from AI models.
- **Not calibrated.** Difficulty, form equivalence, the raw cut (70), and the scaled estimate are assumptions. The app says so.
- **Reading load.** Items average 119 words with options (median 119, maximum 154). At 47 seconds each that is about 150 words a minute before thinking, possibly heavier than the real exam.
- **Style.** Some stems include a clause that rules out one distractor. Round 3 deliberately makes the less idealized option right in many items, so treat the rationale, not the option's tone, as the lesson.
- **Referral is rarely the keyed option** by name (0.3% contain "refer"); it is keyed in substance in K-V-002, J-V-001, A-I-011, B-II-020, D-VI-003 and E-VI-014, among others. The mandated abuse report and imminent-suicide-risk actions are keyed in the drill pool (E-VI-023, E-VI-024). Form A tests what to say before a disclosure; neither form keys the report itself.
- **Ethics Code, SafeSport and MAAPP** text could not be read offline. Standard titles are descriptive and not numbered; check current documents, especially barter, testimonials, gifts, post-termination relationships, and MAAPP messaging and one-on-one rules.
- **References** omit volume, issue and pages because they could not be checked offline. Earlier drafts listed them from memory; they were removed. Seven AASP-list texts are left out because their titles could not be confirmed.
- **Devices.** No physical iPhone or iPad was used. WebKit on Linux approximates Safari; Home Screen install, the share sheet, wake lock, and Safari's storage eviction are unverified on hardware.
- **Commits.** Content and code are separate except for three early content commits that also carry a small script edit.
