## Are the keys right? (blind check)

No human expert has reviewed this bank. Every check below was done by AI models, some from the same family that wrote the items, so shared mistakes can survive.

| Run | Reader | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Round 1 and round 2 drafts | Opus, told the main ethics rules, real item ids | 361/361 and 362/362. Superseded: those items were rewritten |
| **Final bank (364), anonymous ids, no hints** | **Opus** (same family as the writers) | **364/364** |
| **Same** | **Sonnet** (different model) | **348/364 = 95.6%** (±2.1) |

The brief's bar is 95%. Sonnet's number is the more independent one and clears it narrowly.

After that run, 22 items were edited for disagreement, low confidence, or factual/safety notes, and two that drew doubt twice (H-III-004, H-III-008) were replaced. Rechecks: Opus 32/32 and 4/4; Sonnet 30/32 and 3/4. Asking the same model again is weak evidence, so it doesn't change the headline. The last miss (D-II-001) came from an ambiguous quote, since reworded and not re-run. Eight items Sonnet missed were left unedited because the stem decides them: G-II-001, I-I-005, C-I-001, L-I-019, L-I-017, I-IV-032, I-II-003, G-III-015. E-VI-012 was answered correctly but with low confidence. **Treat these, and L-I-014, as the first ones to question.**

Reviewer transcripts were searched for access to keys, the repository, or other packets. None was found (four matches were false positives). Reviewers were kept away by instruction, not by a sandbox.

## Can the keys be guessed without the question? (options-only check)

A reader sees only the four options. Chance is 25%.

| Version | Reader | Keys guessed |
|---|---|---|
| Round 1 | Opus, real ids | 98.3% |
| Round 2 (distractors reworded) | Opus, real ids | 97.5% |
| Round 2, 120-item sample, anonymous ids | Haiku / Sonnet | 88% / 92% |
| Round 3 pilot, same 51 items before → after | Haiku | 80% → 25% |
| | Sonnet | 82% → 27% |
| **Final bank, 364 items** | **Haiku** | **24.5%** (±4.4) |
| **Final bank** | **Sonnet** | 27.5% (second half, 182) and 30.8% (first half, rerun) |

Round 2 failed even though every file scored 0% on the validator's wording heuristics, so a clean cue check in the validator does **not** mean an item is safe from guessing. Round 3 worked by requiring that each option be the best answer to some nearby question (`docs/REVISION_BRIEF_R3.md`). One first-half Sonnet run was discarded and rerun because its answers slipped out of order. By knowledge group, Haiku ranged from 8% (J) to 42% (M, 14 items); B (38%) and M have the most remaining cueing.

## Known limitations

- **No human expert review**, and all blind-check figures are from AI models.
- **Not calibrated.** Difficulty, form equivalence, the raw cut (70), and the scaled estimate are assumptions. The app says so.
- **Reading load.** Items average 119 words with options (median 119, maximum 154). At 47 seconds each that is about 150 words a minute before thinking, possibly heavier than the real exam.
- **Style.** Some stems include a clause that rules out one distractor. Round 3 deliberately makes the less idealized option right in many items, so treat the rationale, not the option's tone, as the lesson.
- **Referral is rarely the keyed option** by name (0.3% contain "refer"); it is keyed in substance in K-V-002, J-V-001, A-I-011, B-II-020, D-VI-003 and E-VI-014, among others. The mandated abuse report and imminent-suicide-risk actions are keyed in the drill pool (E-VI-023, E-VI-024). Form A tests what to say before a disclosure; neither form keys the report itself.
- **Ethics Code, SafeSport and MAAPP** text could not be read offline. Standard titles are descriptive and not numbered; check current documents, especially barter, testimonials, gifts, post-termination relationships, and MAAPP messaging and one-on-one rules.
- **References** omit volume, issue and pages because they could not be checked offline. Earlier drafts listed them from memory; they were removed. Seven AASP-list texts are left out because their titles could not be confirmed.
- **Devices.** No physical iPhone or iPad was used. WebKit on Linux approximates Safari; Home Screen install, the share sheet, wake lock, and Safari's storage eviction are unverified on hardware.
- **Commits.** Content and code are separate except for three early content commits that also carry a small script edit.
